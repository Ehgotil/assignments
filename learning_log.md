# Learning Log

## Week 1
In this week I learned the fundamentals of what an Arduino is (the one we are using is an 8-bit AVR microcontroller with 32KB Memory) and what programming language we will use in the ArduinoIDE to interface with it (C++).
Other components were also introduced such as breadboards and resistors. I used this newfound knowledge to make an LED light up on a breadboard connected to an Arduino.

## Week 2
In this week I learned about resistors and what Ohms are used for. Additionally I learned the importance of color-coding jumper wires. 
<img width="1246" height="1196" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5f040439-042c-4054-b637-f393eedbfdd5" />
(Traditionally one uses red wires for "+" and black wires for "-".) In the same spirit of good practice one should never directly connect the "+" and the "-", due to the fact that this could lead to a damaging short circuit.
I also learned about the GND (ground contact) which acts as a zero-volt reference and return for the current.
In the exercise we experimented with series circuits, parallel circuits and coloured LEDs.

## Week 3
I learned about fading an LED in and out using analogWrite() and use delay() to control the speed of fading. I also learned more about analog signals and their distinction to digital signals.
<img width="1186" height="924" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ec10dc3a-18d2-44c8-a661-02951d1fe568" />
The tilt switch was also introduced as a component that can detect the tilting of an object. Furthermore Serial Baud Rates were introduced. The most common rate for Arduinos is 9600 bps.
We also experimented with DigitalRead() and learned about the Arduinos built-in ADC (Analog to Digital converter).
Then we tested a potentiometer, which is an adjustable resistor and integrated it with AnalogWrite().
<img width="666" height="934" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ba0ec9f7-1a31-47f0-8ef0-50cb2b71143b" />
I also learned about voltage dividers and tried to make a photoresistor work.
